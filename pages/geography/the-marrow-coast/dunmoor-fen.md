South of Port Haldane the Marrow loses its nerve. The fen is nine leagues of reed, black water and drowned hedgerows, with here and there a hill the size of a barn that turns out to be an old house roof.

People do live here: eel-catchers, peat cutters and a handful of hermits who came to hear the sea better. They keep their own bell-lines, strings of copper tubes that ring when the water rises, and they consider the wardens' bells a rather showy way to do something quietly.

> I have lived in the fen forty years and I have never once been lost. The fen has lost *me* a few times, but it always gives me back.
>
> An eel-catcher, quoted by the wardens' census

## Hazards

1. Sinking ground between the reed-islands.
2. Marsh lights, which are only gas, mostly.
3. The things that follow the lights, which are not.
